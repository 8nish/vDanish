Which NSX services live on the edges, and the design choices that come with them.

Goals

  • Identify services hosted on edge nodes versus host transport nodes
  • Understand VRF Lite and its use cases
  • Compare NAT, DNS and DHCP options and their limits
  • Learn IPsec VPN fundamentals

What I covered

  • Centralized gateway services and VRF Lite topology.
  • NAT placement, HA modes, stateful active-active NAT and NAT64.
  • DNS forwarding, and DHCP relay, gateway and segment services.
  • Load balancer components, plus an introduction to IPsec and Layer 2 VPN.

Lessons learned

  • VRF Lite cuts duplicate gateway infrastructure, but VRFs share edge uplink bandwidth.
  • VRF designs must account for the lack of adjacency between SRs, or traffic gets black-holed.
  • Stateful services on a Tier-0 may require active-standby, which means no upstream ECMP.
  • Putting NAT on a Tier-1 keeps the Tier-0 active-active, but can push east-west traffic through the Tier-1 SR.
One-way doorStateful active-active NAT needs an interface group and can't be undone. Changing it later means rebuilding the edge cluster.

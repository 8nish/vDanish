Building a Tier-1 for VM-to-VM traffic, connecting it to a Tier-0 for the outside world, and then fixing it when it didn't work.

Goals

  • Understand how Tier-1 and Tier-0 connect VMs to each other and to outside networks
  • Learn ways to troubleshoot routing and tunnels in NSX

What I covered

  • Set up a Tier-1 for VM-to-VM traffic and connected it to a Tier-0 for external access.
  • Used Traceflow, ping, routing tables and alarms to find problems.
  • Scenario: no external access because the Tier-1 wasn't advertising its networks and the Tier-0 wasn't passing them to the physical router.
  • Drew my own troubleshooting flowcharts for east-west and north-south routing.

Lessons learned

  • A VM can ping its gateway and still fail to reach a VM on another host if the tunnel between the hosts is down.
  • A configured route is not necessarily an active route.
  • For external access, Tier-1 must advertise its networks and Tier-0 must share them with the physical router.

Testing TEPs and MTU with vmkping

# Can this host's TEP reach the other host's TEP?
vmkping ++netstack=vxlan <TEP-IP>

# Can a full 1600-byte packet get through without fragmenting?
vmkping ++netstack=vxlan -d -s 1572 <TEP-IP>

For the payload size, subtract 28 bytes from the MTU you want to test: 20 for the IP header and 8 for ICMP. -d stops fragmentation, so the test only passes if the whole packet fits.

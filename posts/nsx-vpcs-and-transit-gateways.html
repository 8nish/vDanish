VPCs bring self-service, isolated networking to NSX. This day covered how they are built and how traffic leaves them.

Goals

  • Explain VPCs, projects, admin roles and default isolation
  • Compare distributed and centralized transit gateways
  • Identify how private, private-TGW and public subnets behave
  • Choose NAT and connectivity options for common traffic flows

What I covered

  • How VPCs give isolated, self-service networking across projects.
  • Distributed versus centralized transit gateways, including routing and services.
  • How each subnet type communicates inside and outside a VPC.
  • NAT options, and validating VPC connectivity in vCenter.

Lessons learned

  • VPCs in different projects are isolated unless explicitly connected.
  • A distributed transit gateway needs the same external VLAN on every ESX host.
  • Private subnets need NAT or an external IP to reach the outside; public subnets don't.
  • Centralized gateways support custom NAT, and some features need active-standby mode.
